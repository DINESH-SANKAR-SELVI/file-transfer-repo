actually matter.

Strip away the marketing and every software engagement comes down to three questions. Here's how we answer them.

QUESTION 01

Who's actually building it?

With most vendors, you meet the senior team in sales and a junior team after. You have no visibility into who writes the code that runs your business.

The people who sell you the work are the ones who build it.
QUESTION 02

Who owns it when it ships?

Most vendors hand it off and move on. Support becomes a separate team you've never met. Bug fixes become a new project with a new quote.

You own the code from day one — and we keep running it.
QUESTION 03

What happens when it breaks?

Every system breaks eventually. The question is whether the person who fixes it knows the system, or is reading it for the first time at 2 AM.

The team that built it is the team that supports it.

When we're not the right fit.

A "why choose us" page that says we're the best option for everyone isn't useful. Here are four scenarios where you should choose someone else — and we'll tell you so on the first call.

💼
You need 20+ engineers tomorrow
We're a 6+ person team. If you need to scale a team of twenty engineers by next month, we can't help. We'll refer you to a larger agency that can.
🎨
You want a marketing website or brochure app
We don't build simple marketing sites. Our strength is operational software — dashboards, POS, monitoring, internal tools. For a static site, a good freelancer will serve you better and cheaper.
⚖️
Your project needs a formal RFP and 5-vendor bidding
We don't participate in lengthy RFP processes. We prefer a discovery call, a scoped plan, and a decision. If your procurement requires formal bidding, we're happy to step aside.
💵
Your total budget is under $5,000
Below that number, we can't deliver the quality of work we stand behind. We'll gladly refer you to a freelancer or smaller studio who can help more efficiently at that budget.
Why we say this openly: every wrong "yes" costs both sides. A project we shouldn't take means you get mediocre work, we get a stressed team, and nobody wins. When we say yes, we want it to be because we're genuinely the right fit — not because we needed the revenue.

We evaluated four vendors before choosing HashVeno. Two of them were cheaper. One was a bigger name. But only HashVeno asked us the hard questions in the first call — the ones that made us realise what we actually needed. Three months in, they're still the ones on the line when something matters.

The questions most buyers ask us.

Before you get on a call, these are the four things we usually end up discussing anyway.

For a mature product company with a long roadmap, in-house is usually the right answer — and we'll tell you that when it's true. But for most businesses, the math is worse than it looks.

A single senior engineer in Chennai costs ₹20–35 lakhs per year fully loaded. To actually ship and support a production system you need at least three people (frontend, backend, ops). That's ₹60 lakhs+ per year, plus hiring time of 3 months, plus the risk of one person leaving and taking all context with them.

In-house wins when: you have ongoing product work forever, you already have engineering leadership, and you want long-term ownership of the team.

Big agencies have real strengths: large teams, established processes, and the ability to staff a 20-person project quickly. If you need that, you should go there.

What they usually can't offer is continuity. The senior team that pitches the work is rarely the team that builds it. Your project is one of thirty in the pipeline. And after launch, support becomes a ticket queue — not the people who actually know your system.

Agencies win when: you need scale fast, need a wide range of skills, or have a budget that requires a bigger organisation for procurement reasons.

If a good SaaS product already does what you need, use it. It's cheaper, faster, and someone else handles the bugs. We're not here to convince you to build custom software when off-the-shelf works.

But there's a reason we exist. Most businesses eventually hit one of these walls: the SaaS doesn't fit your workflow, the pricing scales faster than your revenue, you can't integrate it with your other tools, or your data ends up locked in a system you don't control.

SaaS wins when: your needs match its assumptions closely, you want zero maintenance burden, and you're comfortable with data living in someone else's infrastructure.

Then we'll tell you on the first call. We keep a short list of trusted freelancers, studios, and specialists we refer work to when it's outside our wheelhouse. We don't try to shoehorn projects into our model, and we don't take work just to book the revenue.

The referrals we make usually come back to us later as clients with work that does fit. So there's no incentive for us to be anything but honest.
