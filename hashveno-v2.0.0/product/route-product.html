Questions, answered.

No. HashRoute is built to integrate with the GPS units and ETM systems already deployed across most state transport corporations. Our integration layer reads standard data formats from your existing devices and streams them into the HashRoute dashboard. If your fleet uses a specific legacy format, our team handles the adapter during setup.
HashRoute compares the number of tickets issued on the ETM against the actual number of passengers detected on board at each stop. When the gap crosses a configurable threshold, the conductor receives an alert on their app to reconcile the count. The depot manager sees the same alert in their dashboard, with full context — route, stop, time, and bus.
HashRoute runs in low-bandwidth mode by default. The on-bus device buffers data locally and syncs whenever a connection is available — with no duplicates and no data loss. Critical alerts (overcrowding, off-route, fare anomaly) are prioritized and sent as compressed packets, so they arrive even on 2G connections.
A single-depot pilot is typically live within 10 days of sign-off — including GPS/ETM integration, conductor app roll-out, and training. A full depot deployment with 100+ buses usually takes 4–6 weeks. Corporation-wide rollouts are phased depot by depot over several months with a dedicated project team.
Yes. City routes, suburban routes, and mofussil (intercity) services all run on the same platform. Route configuration and alert thresholds are fully customisable — a crowded peak-hour city route has different rules than a long-distance mofussil service running through multiple districts.
Yes. HashRoute exposes a documented REST API and webhooks for real-time event streaming. Most corporations integrate via API for their existing control room dashboards, or use HashRoute's own dashboard as the primary interface. SSO and role-based access integrate with standard enterprise identity providers.
